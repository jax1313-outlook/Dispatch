### Ownership questions resolved

- One record, one identity. The card changes name at Accept and carries its
  encoded data end to end. Confirms existing doctrine rather than conflicting
- Worker constitutions do not replace the deterministic chassis. Workers replace
  organizational departments, not mechanisms. Scoring, sorting and
  recommendations stay deterministic. Decision stays human
- Role and implementation are different things. A constitutional role may be
  required while any single implementation of it stays replaceable and optional
- Intelligence owns the opportunity card regardless of which screen displays it
- Accounting is architecturally present as an external wheel. Dispatch prepares
  and routes the handoff; external software owns the accounting record

### Ownership questions still open

- Which screen displays the Intelligence product
- Whether Alerts stays standalone, becomes a Dispatch subsection, or folds into
  a larger review screen
- Whether Publisher absorbs its two inputs
- Agent Level, undetermined

## 8. Executive summary

### What the tab walk removed

Nine entries from the navigation, a quarter of the portal's surface. Nothing was
deleted. Every parked screen still answers at its own path, and every one is a
single line to restore.

What actually went was noise on Home: six counts, a cross-department attention
feed, two charts, an activity feed and a financial snapshot. Home now carries
two numbers and the cards.

The rest were not removals but relocations and boundaries. Fleet moved inside
Settings. SAM was separated toward its own program. Accounting was recognised as
an external wheel rather than Dispatch's work. Billing and Profitability were
parked because they duplicate that wheel.

### What it revealed

**Screens were holding work that belonged to workers.** Opening the Dispatch tab
creates records and raises conflict notices. A calculator wrote expenses onto
live loads. This is the same defect in several places: a page view acting as a
trigger.

**Doctrine is not capability.** Doctrine named an approved mailbox and the code
held eleven notification builders, and neither fact meant a single alert had
ever been delivered. Detection is proven, generation partially, delivery and
tracking not at all. That standard now governs the rest of the walk.

**Two programs were tangled at the root.** Dispatch got its mail transport from
inside the contract engine, and a fuel-receipt scanner sat there too. Both are
resolved. Two more crossings remain: one shared data store and one backup path.

**Duplication was structural, not cosmetic.** The opportunity card existed as
four hand-copied blocks. The financial figures render on three screens. Billing
and Profitability answer nearly the same question as each other, and both
duplicate an external system.

**The naming was wrong in a way that hid the model.** Operations was not
operations. Fleet was not a tab. Active Cards were not active. Each rename
exposed an ownership question that the old name had concealed.

### What now appears to be the true operational core

On the evidence so far, and reported rather than recommended, Dispatch's
operational core is narrower than its navigation ever suggested. It is:

**One record, moving.** Screened opportunity, human Accept, committed load,
mission, archive. One identity throughout.

**Three things around it.** What is coming, what is committed, and what has
fallen outside normal operations.

**One place the shop is configured.** Settings, now holding the roster.

Everything else is either an input to that spine, a utility beside it, or work
that belongs to an external wheel. The walk has not contradicted that shape
once, and four tabs remain unwalked to test it.
